## The entry (146 characters, 1 sentence, 28 words)

> The SnitchPlant is a houseplant that, when you forget to water it, texts your mum a photo of itself wilting with the caption 'you did this to me.'

## Method — why this pitch, against the judge's brief (in its order)

1. **On brief:** one sentence pitching a ridiculous invention (a houseplant that snitches) fixing a tiny everyday annoyance (forgetting to water it).
2. **Funny from the invention itself:** the laugh is the plant running a blackmail operation on its owner — not a joke bolted on at the end. No meta text addressed to the judge; the pitch stands on its own.
3. **Clever fix:** the plant outsources its watering to maternal guilt — silly, yet weirdly plausible as a reminder system.
4. **Original:** a plant as informant. Deliberately not the sponsor's own examples (self-apologising alarm clock, sock filing a formal complaint, sighing kettle) and not a sighing/complaint-filing appliance.
5. **Tight:** annoyance, invention and silly mechanism in one clean sentence, 146 of the 220 allowed characters. Short text also wins exact ties (fewer bytes ranks first).

Seven candidate pitches were drafted and scored against the five criteria above (see the builder's notes); the SnitchPlant won on every criterion. Alternatives considered and rejected: a guilt-tripping decaf mug (familiar grandmother-voice trope), a self-hanging revenge towel (revenge, not remedy), an honest smoke alarm that only screams at bad cooking (trades one problem for a worse one), a dial-up-throttling bedtime router (doomscrolling is a well-worn annoyance).

## Run it
